# Canon and recency: best-regarded and newest talks on AI/LLM evals (2023 to 17 Nov 2026)

Method notes for the report writer. View counts, publish dates and lengths were read from each YouTube watch page's own metadata on 2026-11-17; "pub." means YouTube publish date, which is often weeks or months after the event. Theses for AI Engineer talks come from the organiser's talk pages (ai.engineer/talks/...), which are written summaries, not transcripts. No published "best eval talks" ranking exists, so the rank order below is my composite (see Inferences under the first question). Anything marked "unverified" was seen only in a search snippet or secondary write-up.

## 1. Which eval talks are most recommended in curated lists, newsletters, course syllabi and roundups?

### Takeaway
There is no authoritative ranked list; the closest thing to a canon is the benchflow-ai/awesome-evals talks section (77 entries), Hamel Husain's own education pages, and the AI Engineer conference eval tracks. Across those, Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar are the most cross-cited speakers by a wide margin, and AI Engineer (World's Fair and Summit) is the dominant venue; OpenAI DevDay, NeurIPS tutorials and Stanford/Berkeley lectures supply the rest.

### Inferences
- Rank order is mine: cross-citation in curated lists first, then view count, then how central evals are to the talk, with a deliberate spread across practitioner, agent, academic and dissenting material. A pure view-count order is given under question 2.
- The canon is narrow in authorship: five of the 24 entries involve Husain and/or Shankar, and awesome-evals lists six further talks or podcasts by them plus their FAQ essay. A new talk that repeats "look at your data, error analysis, validate your judge" will be heard as a restatement of their material.
- AI Engineer is where eval talks get an audience; the same topic at PyData or QCon draws two to three orders of magnitude fewer recorded views.

### Gaps
- No ranked "best eval talks" roundup by a newsletter was found; Latent Space, Pragmatic Engineer and Simon Willison publish eval essays, not talk rankings.
- I found no eval-specific session at OpenAI DevDay 2026 (keynote pub. Sep 29, 2026) or Google I/O 2026 in YouTube title searches; absence in a title search is not proof of absence.
- No evaluation-themed keynote was confirmed at ICML, ICLR or ACL 2023 to 2026; a search summary mentions an ICML 2026 workshop keynote on DARPA's AIQ programme (unverified). Strange Loop and GOTO returned nothing relevant.
- awesome-evals venue labels are sometimes wrong (e.g. it tags talks published in April to May 2026 as "World's Fair 2025"); I did not resolve which event each was recorded at.

## 3. What is new between June and November 2026?

### Takeaway
The period's news is consolidation and a shift of emphasis from offline eval suites to closed loops, harnesses and production monitoring, alongside a benchmark-integrity scare (METR on GPT-5.6 Sol) and OpenAI retiring its hosted Evals product. The "Claude Code has no evals" talking point was publicly contradicted in July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Jun 3, 2026: OpenAI announced deprecation of its Evals platform (dashboard, API, graders) and Agent Builder; status "Read-only", shutdown Nov 30, 2026; recommended path "Migrate to Promptfoo". A search summary of OpenAI's guide gives Oct 31, 2026 as the read-only date. — [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)
- Jun 6, 2026: Ara Khan (Cline), "Evals Are Broken, Use Them Anyway" (19:04; 6,107 views): Cline started at 43% on Terminal Bench and improved through container settings, timeouts and model-family-specific prompting, not a better model. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuuIywMG4s8)
- Jun 10, 2026: Luke Lin, "The Return of the Eval": evals came back as telemetry for agents; "Yes, we need evals. No, we don't need over-built golden datasets." — [The AI Craft](https://pmdata.substack.com/p/the-return-of-the-eval)

## 4. What recurring themes appear across the top talks, and which dissenting talks are notable?

### Takeaway
Five themes recur in most of the top talks: look at real traces first, generic metrics fail, validate the LLM judge against humans, public benchmarks do not predict your product, and evals belong in a loop with production. The notable dissent is about framing (evals are not unit tests; static evals are the wrong shape for agents), not about whether to measure.

### Cited Findings
- Error analysis before metrics: "Always start with error analysis (don't jump into writing evals)". — [Lenny's show notes](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-evals-are-the-hottest-new-skill)
- Generic metrics and unverified judges are the first two of Husain's five pitfalls; he also prefers binary pass/fail to Likert scales. — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/revenge/)
- Judge calibration against human labels: Kumar reaches 27/30 agreement and gates at 80%; Uber grades its router with a confusion matrix against human labels. — [Kumar](https://ai.engineer/talks/NdrSPm6NCdk-evals-in-ai-a-deep-dive); [Uber](https://ai.engineer/talks/31GUkCBD-Uc-building-closed-loop-evals-multimodal-agent)
- Benchmarks versus reality: Kapoor notes Devin succeeded at 3 of 20 tasks despite strong SWE-bench results; Fowler's article says benchmarks measure general quality, so build task-specific datasets. — [Kapoor](https://ai.engineer/talks/d5EltXhbcfA-building-and-evaluating-ai-agents); [Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/gen-ai-patterns/)
- Loop/flywheel: Vercel's "AI-native flywheel"; Hetzel's production-to-offline flywheel; Uber's diagnoser-and-autotune loop. — [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/eval-driven-development-build-better-ai-faster); [Hetzel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms)
- Non-determinism and reliability: Kapoor separates capability (pass@k) from reliability; Anthropic defines pass^k and says "we run multiple trials"; Schmid recommends 3 to 6 trials per case. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); [Schmid](https://ai.engineer/talks/0vphxNt4wyk-dont-ship-skills-without-evals)
- Goodhart, gaming and leakage: Heiner on public test data leaking into training and undisclosed private testing; Kapoor's CUDA-kernel reward-hacking example; Uber's agent becoming "overly conservative in response to QA". — [Heiner](https://ai.engineer/talks/-npY6XjM8CQ-when-will-benchmaxxing-plague-end); [Kapoor](https://ai.engineer/talks/d5EltXhbcfA-building-and-evaluating-ai-agents); [Uber](https://ai.engineer/talks/31GUkCBD-Uc-building-closed-loop-evals-multimodal-agent)
- Evals as specification owned by domain experts: "Evals are the new PRD" (Ankur Goyal, How I AI, Jun 15, 2026; 9,270 views; also a Dianne Penn/Anthropic clip, Aug 5, 2026; 22,734 views); Chime's "Make Legal Write Your Evals". — [Goyal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QE_1hRLsehM); [Penn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gjGxEQgb7Xw)
- Dissent, Sept 2025: Helicone's CEO ("f*** evals!") and AgentOps' CEO ("Evals are a scam"); LangWatch concluded "The reports of Evals' death were greatly exaggerated." — [LangWatch](https://langwatch.ai/blog/are-evals-dead)
- Dissent on shape: Vincent Koc, "Malleable Evals: Why Are We Evaluating Adaptive Systems with Static Tests?" (pub. May 12, 2026; 15:05; 11,342 views); Anshuman Mishra, "Evals are dead. Long live Evals." (Feb 19, 2026) arguing for interactive environments. — [Koc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VhbYlfC7Gs); [Mishra](https://onlyagents.substack.com/p/evals-are-dead-long-live-evals)
- Dissent on the testing frame: Pesok and Hetzel (entries 7 and 11 above); Kumar warns perfect agreement can signal overfitting, and that passing a gate "establishes agreement on the collected cases, not complete production coverage". — [Kumar](https://ai.engineer/talks/NdrSPm6NCdk-evals-in-ai-a-deep-dive)
- Dissent on scope: Hamel Husain vs Han-Chung Lee at Data Council 2025 ("The Model is Not the Product" vs "The Model is the Product"), paired in awesome-evals. — [awesome-evals](https://github.com/benchflow-ai/awesome-evals)

### Inferences
- The deck's four "where the analogy breaks" items map onto themes that each have a strong talk behind them (noise: Wang, Kapoor; no spec: Pragmatic Engineer, Penn/Goyal; Goodhart: Heiner, Ratner, METR; leakage: Heiner, SWE-bench Verified), but I found no single talk that presents them together as a list of failures of the testing analogy.

## 5. Structurally, what do the best-received eval talks do?

### Takeaway
They are short (15 to 22 minutes), open with a concrete failure or a show of hands, anchor on one named product case, and leave the audience with a small numbered procedure or a named framework. Before/after numbers help but are not required: the most-viewed 2026 eval talk reports none.

### Cited Findings
- Opening with an audience poll or a live failure: Schmid asks who uses skills, then who has evals for them; Kumar opens with a prompt-injected HTML comment and then makes `expect(answer).toContain("cannot")` pass on a wrong answer; Pesok's fruit-letter-counting app works in the demo and fails after deployment. — [Schmid](https://ai.engineer/talks/0vphxNt4wyk-dont-ship-skills-without-evals); [Kumar](https://ai.engineer/talks/NdrSPm6NCdk-evals-in-ai-a-deep-dive); [Pesok](https://ai.engineer/talks/evals-are-not-unit-tests)
- Real walkthroughs: the Lenny episode reviews real traces from a property-management assistant on screen; Husain & Sedgh walk through one product's eval system. — [Lenny's show notes](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-evals-are-the-hottest-new-skill); [Husain & Sedgh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eLXF0VojuSs)
- Numbers that stick: Kapoor's $57 vs $664 for comparable accuracy, fewer than 40% of papers reproduced, 3 of 20 tasks; Schmid's 33.9% to 50.5% across 87 tasks and 117 test cases; the Kaggle team's 22% harness swing; Cline's 43% start. — [Kapoor](https://ai.engineer/talks/d5EltXhbcfA-building-and-evaluating-ai-agents); [Kaggle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA)
- Named frameworks and metaphors: three levels of evals (Husain); AI-native flywheel (Vercel); four maturity phases (Hetzel); five lessons/five pitfalls (Goyal; Shankar & Husain); basketball-court map of the input domain (Pesok); Swiss-cheese layers (Uber); "two pedals on a bicycle" (Kumar); Easy/Hard/God mode (Viv Trivedy, 37,482 views). — [Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/); [Trivedy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zLeG-XJtbIE)
- Homework close: Schmid ends by asking the audience to pick their most-used skill, write five test prompts and run with and without the skill. — [Schmid](https://ai.engineer/talks/0vphxNt4wyk-dont-ship-skills-without-evals)
- Uber's talk gives eight design steps and six pitfalls but "reports no before/after results, agreement rates, or conversion lift". — [Uber talk page](https://ai.engineer/talks/31GUkCBD-Uc-building-closed-loop-evals-multimodal-agent)
- Lengths of the top short-form talks: Kapoor 19:59, Uber 21:38, Schmid 21:45, Husain & Sedgh 18:44, Hetzel 18:33, Pesok 15:21 (each from its YouTube page, linked in the shortlist under question 1). — [Kapoor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d5EltXhbcfA); [Uber](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=31GUkCBD-Uc); [Schmid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vphxNt4wyk)

### Inferences
- The repeatable recipe: provocative title, 60-second failure demo, one case with a recognisable brand, three to five numbered moves, an honest limit, and a task the audience can do on Monday.
- Stating limits earns trust: Kumar flags thresholds as operating choices and Cat Wu admits incomplete confidence; neither weakens the talk.

### Gaps
- "Best-received" is inferred from views and list inclusion; I found no audience-rating data for any conference.

## 6. Which existing talks or essays already use a software-testing framing for evals?

### Takeaway
The testing analogy is well-trodden: it appears as levels (unit tests, human/model eval, A/B), as a testing pyramid, as eval-driven development, and as CI gates. The two best-known talks on it argue against a naive mapping. None of the sources reviewed uses Google's Testing on the Toilet format as the vehicle, but I did not search for that directly (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- Hamel Husain, "Your AI Product Needs Evals" (Mar 29, 2024): Level 1 is "Unit Tests ... assertions (like you would write in pytest)", run in CI; but "you don't necessarily need a 100% pass rate". — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/)
- Vercel, "Eval-driven development: Build better AI faster" (Oct 17, 2024): evals "are like end-to-end tests for AI and other probabilistic systems". — [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/eval-driven-development-build-better-ai-faster)
- Ido Pesok, "Evals Are Not Unit Tests" (2025): unit and end-to-end tests still matter but do not cover model behaviour. — [talk page](https://ai.engineer/talks/evals-are-not-unit-tests)
- Subramaniam & Fowler, "Emerging Patterns in Building GenAI Products" (Feb 25, 2025): "we treat evals similarly to how we work with performance testing"; "Unlike tests, they aren't simple binary pass/fail results, instead we have to set thresholds." — [martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/gen-ai-patterns/)
- Pragmatic Engineer (Dec 2, 2025): "TDD works because for a given input, there is a single, deterministic, knowable, correct output to assert against"; code-based evals "mirror traditional unit testing". — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals)
- Google Cloud Tech, "How to evaluate agents in practice" (Dec 12, 2025; 10:53; 60,270 views): a "3-Tier Testing Pyramid" of component-level unit tests, trajectory-level integration tests and human review. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vuBvf7ZRKTA)
- Anthropic, "Demystifying evals for AI agents" (Jan 9, 2026): owning evals "should be as routine as maintaining unit tests"; capability evals vs regression evals; "regression tests for free"; run in CI/CD; practise eval-driven development. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- Anita (Vellum), "AI Agents, Meet Test Driven Development" (AI Engineer; pub. Feb 22, 2025; 29:10; 14,012 views). — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U3MVU6JpocU)
- 2026 additions: Hetzel (unit-test frame is wrong); Schmid ("You wouldn't merge code without tests"); Kumar (assertions fail live); DeLucia ("how it differs from a unit test"); Mastra, "You'll Finally Understand AI Evals After Watching This" on eval vs test (Jun 18, 2026; 22,721 views); Urs Peter, "Eval-Driven Development" (KotlinConf, pub. Jul 11, 2026; 4,359 views); SonderMind's "Evals-Driven Development"; UiPath, "Evaluation-driven development for enterprise AI agents" (25,605 views); Langfuse's CI gate. — [Mastra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=12WN6u2DrBk); [Peter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2bZzPXfmyE); [UiPath](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KxJKKeib94); [DeLucia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gv4w23xYj-0); [Langfuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=obfPzbapMIM); [Hetzel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FB-MLPhL9Ms); [SonderMind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA)
- Low-reach pieces with the literal framing: Aakash Gupta, "Why AI Evals Are the New Unit Tests" (Medium; search result only) and Vijay Rangan, "Evals Are Just TDD for AI" (8 views). — [Gupta](https://aakashgupta.medium.com/why-ai-evals-are-the-new-unit-tests-the-quality-assurance-revolution-in-genai-456888217342); [YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=test+driven+development+LLM+evals)

### Inferences
- "Evals are to models what tests are to software" is, on its own, not new, and the audience may already have heard the rebuttal. Three things were absent from the sources reviewed: (a) the Testing on the Toilet delivery format (not directly searched); (b) a single explicit list of where the analogy breaks (noise, no spec, Goodhart, leakage); (c) a hard release rule stated as a slogan. "No evals, no deploy" is closest to Schmid's title and to the CI-gate demos by Langfuse and Kumar, so the deck should cite or outdo them (for example by showing the gate, threshold and trial count).
- The unit/integration/regression mapping overlaps Google's testing pyramid and Anthropic's capability-vs-regression split; differentiation would come from mapping eval types to test types and then showing what each mapping gets wrong.
- Given 2026 emphasis, a deck that stops at offline suites will look dated; one slide connecting the gate to production traces and monitoring would align it with current talks.

### Gaps
- I did not search for Testing on the Toilet applied to evals, because that query would in effect disclose the deck's title; whether anyone has used that format is unverified.
- Essay dates for the Gupta Medium post and other "evals vs software testing" blog posts in search results were not opened or verified.
